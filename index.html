Hide.Code.Mail

A Gmail® (not affiliated) address ignores every dot and every letter case you put in it. That unused space is enough to carry a short tag, hidden in plain sight, with nothing stored anywhere.

piero.dellafrancesca@gmail.com, pIero.dellaFrancesca@gmail.com and pi.ero.della.francesca@gmail.com all land in the same inbox. Hide.Code.Mail uses exactly that redundancy: it reads the pattern of dots and capital letters as a sequence of yes/no answers, and turns that sequence into a short tag, or back again. Nothing is saved, not on this page and not anywhere else: the address itself, exactly as typed with its dots and its capital letters, is all that is ever needed to reconstruct the hidden string.

How the encoding works

  1. The address is reduced to its canonical form: every dot removed, everything lowercase. That is the address Gmail® actually sees.
  2. Each letter gets one slot for its case, and each gap between two characters gets one slot for an optional dot. A longer address means more slots.
  3. The tag is converted into a single number, then into a string of 0s and 1s exactly as long as the number of slots available.
  4. A 1 in a case slot means that letter is written uppercase; a 1 in a gap slot means a dot is inserted there. Everything else stays as it was.
  5. Reading a tagged address just runs the same steps backwards: record each letter's case and each gap's dot, rebuild the number, rebuild the tag.

Full derivation, edge cases and the reasoning behind each design choice are written out in ALGORITHM.md.
